Machine Learning Applications in Climate Change Prediction Models

1. Introduction

¶1 Climate prediction has historically relied on physically based general circulation models whose accuracy is bounded by the resolution at which sub-grid processes such as cloud formation can be represented (Randall et al., 2007). Over the last decade, the volume of satellite and reanalysis data has grown faster than the computational budgets available to assimilate it.

B I U Cite Comment Ask AI AI This thesis asks whether data-driven models can complement, rather than replace, process-based simulation. Recent work suggests that deep learning architectures trained on reanalysis products can learn stable emulators of atmospheric dynamics, offering orders-of-magnitude speed-ups at inference time while preserving physically plausible behaviour over medium-range horizons (Reichstein et al., 2019).

Machine Learning Applications in Climate Change Prediction Models

1. Introduction

Climate prediction has historically relied on physically based general circulation models whose accuracy is bounded by the resolution at which sub-grid processes such as cloud formation can be represented (Randall et al., 2007). Over the last decade, the volume of satellite and reanalysis data has grown faster than the computational budgets available to assimilate it.

This thesis asks whether data-driven models can complement, rather than replace, process-based simulation. Recent work suggests that deep learning architectures trained on reanalysis products can learn stable emulators of atmospheric dynamics, offering orders-of-magnitude speed-ups at inference time while preserving physically plausible behaviour over medium-range horizons (Reichstein et al., 2019).

The term "hybrid modelling" is used here to describe any configuration in which a learned component is embedded inside, or coupled to, a numerical solver. The remainder of this chapter situates the research question within the literature and states the contributions.
